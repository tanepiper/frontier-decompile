# The Americas

This page is not legal advice. The law of the country where you live applies to you. The United States
is on [its own page](us-europe-uk.md).

Canada has interoperability exceptions both for studying a program and for locks. Mexico makes
circumvention for profit a crime. Brazil and Argentina have no decompilation exception.

## Canada

- **Studying a program.** Section 30.6 of the Copyright Act lets the owner of a program copy adapt
  it where this is essential to run it on a computer, for their own use. Section 30.61, from
  2012, allows copying "for the sole purpose of obtaining information" to make a program
  interoperable
  ([Justice Laws](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-9.html)).
- **Locks.** Section 41.1 bans breaking access controls and dealing in tools. An individual who
  breaks a lock only for private purposes faces no statutory damages. The interoperability
  exception in s.41.12 was widened in November 2024 to cover "a device in which it is embedded",
  and s.41.121 added a repair exception
  ([Justice Laws](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-12.html)).
- **Libraries and archives.** Section 30.1 lets libraries, archives and museums copy works in an
  obsolete format, unless a copy is on sale
  ([Justice Laws](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-8.html)).
- **How long copyright lasts.** The author's life plus 70 years since 30 December 2022. Works whose
  copyright had already ended did not get it back.
- **Owners who cannot be found.** The Copyright Board can license them
  ([Copyright Board](https://www.cb-cda.gc.ca/en/unlocatable-owners)). It granted 277 licences
  from 1990 to 2013, none for games.
- **Cases.** In *Nintendo v. King* (2017), the Federal Court awarded $12.76 million against a
  seller of game copiers and mod chips. The court rejected the defence that buyers used them for
  homebrew, because the seller gave no evidence for it
  ([Smart & Biggar](https://smartbiggar.ca/insights/publication/nintendo-awarded-12-7-million-for-circumvention-of-technological-protection-measures-in-precedent-setting-case)).

## Mexico

- **Studying a program.** Art. 105 of the Federal Copyright Law allows one use or back-up copy
  ([LFDA art. 105](https://mley.mx/LFDA/articulo/105/index.html.md)). The only reverse
  engineering rule is in the lock article.
- **Locks.** Art. 114 Quáter, from July 2020, excuses breaking a lock to make an independently
  created program interoperable
  ([LFDA art. 114 Quáter](https://mley.mx/LFDA/articulo/114-qu%C3%A1ter/index.html.md)).
  **Federal Penal Code art. 427 Bis punishes knowing circumvention for profit with six months to
  six years in prison**
  ([CPF art. 427 bis](https://mley.mx/CPF/articulo/427-bis/index.html.md)). The Supreme Court
  upheld these articles on 3 June 2024.
- **Libraries and archives.** Art. 148 V lets a library make one copy of a work that is out of
  print and at risk of disappearing.
- **How long copyright lasts.** The author's life plus 100 years, the longest term in the world
  ([LFDA art. 29](https://mley.mx/LFDA/articulo/29/index.html.md)).
- **Cases.** The authorities enforce the law by raids. The industrial property office IMPI closed
  Mexico City's Friki Plaza in one of them.

## Brazil

- **Studying a program.** Art. 6 of the Software Law (Lei 9.609 of 1998) lists the only acts that
  do not infringe: a back-up copy, quotations for teaching, similarity forced by function, and
  integration needed for the user's own use. **There is no decompilation or interoperability
  exception** ([Lei 9.609](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9609.htm)).
- **Locks.** Art. 107 of the Copyright Law makes disabling copy controls a civil wrong, with no
  exceptions ([Lei 9.610](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9610.htm)). Brazil is not
  a party to the WIPO Copyright Treaty.
- **Libraries and archives.** No preservation exception.
- **How long copyright lasts.** 50 years from publication for software. Copyright in a game's art,
  music and film parts lasts longer.
- **Games that shut down.** Bill PL 3612/2026 would require two years of support and 180 days'
  notice. After a shutdown, a company would have to offer an offline version, community server
  tools or a partial refund ([Game Overdrive](https://gameoverdrive.com.br/pl-3612-2026-preservacao-jogos/)).
  It has not passed.

## Argentina

- **Studying a program.** Ley 11.723 protects source and object code and excludes "ideas,
  procedures, methods and concepts" as such. Art. 9 allows one back-up copy. **The law has no
  decompilation exception, no lock rule and no library exception**
  ([InfoLeg](http://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/40000-44999/42755/texact.htm)).
- **How long copyright lasts.** The author's life plus 70 years.

## Chile

- **Studying a program.** Art. 71 Ñ of Ley 17.336, from 2010, is the broadest software exception
  in the region. It allows reverse engineering of a lawful copy for interoperability "or for
  research and development purposes"
  ([LeyChile](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=28933)).
- **Locks.** Chile has no circumvention ban.
- **Libraries and archives.** Art. 71 I lets non-profit libraries copy works that have been off
  the market for three years. Art. 71 K allows on-site terminals.
- **How long copyright lasts.** The author's life plus 70 years, or 70 years from publication for
  company works.

## Colombia

- **Studying a program.** Andean Decision 351 allows a copy or adaptation that is indispensable
  for use.
- **Locks.** Ley 1915 of 2018 makes circumvention a civil wrong (art. 12). Art. 13(a) excuses
  reverse engineering for interoperability. The copyright office can add exceptions every three
  years ([Ley 1915](http://www.secretariasenado.gov.co/senado/basedoc/ley_1915_2018.html)).
- **Libraries and archives.** Art. 16 allows lending and on-site terminals.
- **How long copyright lasts.** 70 years from publication for company works.
- **Owners who cannot be found.** Arts. 18 to 21 let institutions use orphan works. Software is
  not named.

## What this means for a project

Canada and Chile permit study of a program. Mexico and Colombia permit it through their lock
exceptions. Brazil and Argentina do not expressly permit decompiling. There, observing the
running game and writing new code that copies no expression carry the least risk. In Mexico, breaking a lock
for profit is a crime.
